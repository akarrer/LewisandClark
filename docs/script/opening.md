# Opening — narration script (draft 1)

The Opening is described in ADR-0008. Line ids (`op.01`…) are the keys the Cutscene shot list will use for voiceover and subtitle cues, so keep them stable when rewording.

**Voice:** Captain Meriwether Lewis in the first person, as if writing by candlelight and reading it back. Formal, private and unhurried. No accent. Let the pauses carry weight: the music and the river fill them.

**Budget:** about 120 spoken words over 85 seconds, at roughly 110 words a minute with room to breathe. Times are targets. Your rough take sets the real cut.

## Title scene (before New Expedition)

No narration. The fiddler plays Soldier's Joy by the fire. You can hear the men around it but not make out words.

## Beat 1 — Black (0:00–0:08)

The fiddle fades. River against the hull, oars in the water.

| id | speaker | line | direction |

| op.01 | Lewis | July the thirtieth, eighteen hundred and four. | Like writing a heading. Even. |
| op.02 | Lewis | Seventy-seven days upon the Missouri. | A little tired. Proud underneath. |

## Beat 2 — Sunrise, the snag (0:08–0:25)

The sun breaks over the keelboat under oars. The boat lurches, men shout, and the setting poles go in.

| id | speaker | line | direction |
|---|---|---|---|
| op.03 | Lewis | The river contests every mile, and hides its snags beneath the mud. | Over the calm, before the lurch. |
| — | Corps | *(shouts, poles, "Heave!")* | Crowd effects, not you. |
| op.04 | Lewis | This morning, we found one. | Dry. The only joke in the Opening. |

## Beat 3 — The map (0:25–0:45)

The aerial shot dissolves into the 1804 map. An ink line runs from St. Louis to the bend below the bluff, then the map ahead goes pale and empty.

| id | speaker | line | direction |
|---|---|---|---|
| op.05 | Lewis | Last spring the President purchased a country no American has seen. | Plain statement. Let "no American" land. |
| op.06 | Lewis | He has asked us to cross it to the western ocean, and set down all we find. | Lift slightly on "western ocean." |
| op.07 | Lewis | Every map I carry falls silent a little way above this bend. | Quiet. The ink line stops on "silent." |

Smoke rises on a far ridge. Control starts as op.13 begins, and the HUD fades in while it plays out.

|---|---|---|---|

## History notes (for the Journal's "what really happened")

- **Date and place:** the Corps came up to Council Bluff on 30 July 1804 and camped below it. The Otoe–Missouria council was held on 3 August. It was a short move that day, so op.03–04 compress the river's difficulty for effect.
- **Whose words:** Lewis's journal has a gap for this period, and the surviving July 1804 entries are Clark's. All of Lewis's narration here is invented in his voice. op.08 paraphrases Clark's own delight at the view from the bluff.
- **Days on the river:** 14 May to 30 July is 77 days.
- **Corps size:** about 45 men, including French boatmen, with the keelboat and two pirogues. "Forty-odd" avoids committing to a number.
- **Dorion:** Pierre Dorion Sr., who lived among the Yankton Sioux, joined the Corps in June 1804 as an interpreter. *Check before recording:* he may not have been the one sent to the Otoe. He was the man who knew the Sioux, which is why he gets the warning.
- **op.11:** historically the council told the Otoe and Missouria that they now had a new "great father" in Washington. "In the President's name" hints at that without having Lewis deliver the speech, and the Council Scenario handles it on screen.

## Alternates to try on the take

- op.03: *"The river gives nothing freely. It hides its snags beneath a skin of mud, and dares us to find them."*
- op.07: *"A little way above this bend, every map I carry turns to guesswork."*
- op.13: *"Forty-odd men, and a long way from anywhere. Let us go and see who has lit that fire."* This one keeps the player's focus on the smoke. Use it if the handoff is aimed at the ridge rather than the council ground.
- op.10: *"Drouillard is already into the trees. He will come back with meat, or with news."* Use it if beat 4 runs long on picture.
