# Species of the Expedition

What the Corps of Discovery recorded, with an eye to what we can put in the game.
Compiled 2026-09-19. Sources at the bottom; **dates and places are the useful part**,
because a sighting only feels real if it happens where and when it really happened.

Headline numbers, often quoted: **178 plants and 122 animals** new to science
(roughly 128 of the plants), 237 of Lewis's pressed plants survive, most of them in
the Philadelphia Herbarium.

## 1. Inside the Vertical Slice (Council Bluff → Teton Sioux, 30 Jul – 25 Sep 1804)

These are the ones we can place honestly in the Slice. Dates are the expedition's own.

### At Council Bluff — the ground the Slice opens on

| Date | Species | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|
| 30 Jul | **American badger** (*Taxidea taxus*) | Joseph Field killed it; **Lewis's first zoological specimen of the expedition**. Clark: "Brarow or as the Ponie call it Cho car tooch, this animale burrows in the ground & feeds on Bugs and flesh principally the little Dogs of the Prarie." Skins went to Jefferson in April 1805. |
| 30 Jul | **Illinois bundleflower** (*Desmanthus illinoensis*) | Lewis's specimen No. 18 (later lost), "in the praries in high situations", 3½–4 feet high. |
| 2 Aug | **Great egret** | Shot at present-day Fort Calhoun — the council ground itself. |
| 3 Aug | *(the council)* | No natural history: Clark only records "The Musquitors more numerous than I ever Saw them." A joke waiting to happen. |
| 5 Aug | **Interior least tern** | Specimen taken in Washington County, Nebraska. Nests on bare sandbars — we have those. |
| 5 Aug | **Wood duck**, **bullsnake** | Ducks on the river; the snake killed near Soldier Creek, Iowa. |
| 8 Aug | **American white pelican** | "A flock of several hundred" on a sandbar near the Little Sioux. Lewis tested a pelican's pouch by pouring water into it (5 gallons). |

### Already in our world, unremarked

Cottonwood, willow (the sandbar thickets), bur oak in the draws, tallgrass,
white-tailed deer, elk, wild turkey, beaver, catfish, mosquitoes. These are the
"common" entries a journal needs so the rare ones read as rare.

## 2. Beyond the Slice (for later regions)

Grizzly bear, swift fox, bushy-tailed woodrat, Columbian ground squirrel,
bighorn sheep, mountain goat, mountain beaver, long-tailed weasel;
trumpeter swan, greater sage-grouse, piping plover, common poorwill,
**Lewis's woodpecker**, McCown's longspur, western meadowlark;
western rattlesnake, western hognose snake, cutthroat trout, blue and channel
catfish, goldeye, mountain sucker; bitterroot (*Lewisia rediviva*), *Clarkia pulchella*,
mock orange (*Philadelphus lewisii*), camas, beargrass, snowberry, Oregon grape,
ponderosa pine, Douglas fir, prickly pear, sagebrush.

Two carry the men's names — *Lewisia*, *Clarkia* — and three became state flowers
(Oregon, Idaho, Montana). Worth saving for the moment the player reaches them.

## 3. Caveats before we ship any of this

- **"Discovered" is the wrong word** and the journals are better than it. Many species
  were long known to the Nations who lived there, and often to French traders; what the
  expedition did was describe them for Eastern science. Our journal entries should say
  "described", and where a Native name is recorded (Oto *Cho car tooch* for badger), use it.
- A few claims float around loose. The badger was already known in Canada; the pronghorn
  was described by the expedition but not strictly new. Mark each entry in the data file as
  `new_to_science: true/false/contested` rather than flattening it.
- The full 178/122 lists live in Moulton's edition and the Reveal herbarium catalogue
  (`plantsystematics.org/reveal/pbio/LnC/`, http only). Worth a pass when we build regions
  beyond the Slice.
